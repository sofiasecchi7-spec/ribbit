# counter

Mobile-first web app: a tally counter, branded **Ribbit**. Design source: Figma file `CGp1QCAYHV2i0suePkeqxx` ("Ribbit"); the Home screen is node `166:124` and was built to match it to the pixel at 402×874.

- **Home (`#home`, what the app opens on):** background: the parchment with the rename screen's blue gradient rising from the bottom — 55% `#7fa8c9` at the bottom edge, easing out (∝ distance²) by 62% of the way up. "Ribbit" title (Sigmar 50px, 16px from the top — raised from the Figma frame's 48px at the user's request) at the top left, 16px from the edge (moved from the centre at the user's request), a green round **+** button bottom-centre (`.icon-button`, `icons/plus.svg`: an 80px green face with a thick dark green rim — 3px around, 7px at the bottom, 86×90 overall, per the user's reference PNG; the pile measures it), and above it a pile of project balls (circles filled with the buttons' cream "white" `#faf4e6`, with the chunky button border — 1px brown, 3px at the bottom — name curved along the bottom edge in Geist SemiBold 20px via an SVG `textPath` on an r=92 circle, drawn in a 204-unit viewBox so it scales with the ball). The newest (most recently updated) ball is full size, 204px; older ones shrink (160px, then ×0.9 each, min 108px). Layout (`pileLayout` in `app.js`) drops balls newest-first into the lowest free spot inside the content area, resting on the floor, a side wall, the + button (a round obstacle, measured from the DOM) or earlier balls, 10px apart — so the newest sits on the + against the left edge and smaller ones fill the gaps. The pile scrolls when it overflows (starts at the bottom; fades out under the title over 24px — the title area stays solid; a progressive blur there, and later an Apple Watch-style bubble layout, were tried and reverted at the user's request). Long names shrink to 14px, then truncate. Each ball shows a preview of its project's dots: the middle of the pile laid out as in the counter strip (12px dots — not scaled with the ball — same gaps, newest row on top, the project's dot colour), centred and clipped crisply at the inside edge of the border; the name sits on a solid label on top of the dots — a cream tab rising from the ball's rim, as wide as the name plus 10 units each side: 1px brown line on its top edge (an arc just above the letters) and its two (radial) sides only, rounded top corners, and rounded bottom corners too (rounding inward, meeting the rim tangentially), its bottom running into the ball's border — it's clipped at the inside edge of the border like the dots (`drawBand`) (`ballPreview` in `app.js` only builds the rows/dots that can show). Tap a ball = open its counter; **+** = new project; long-press a ball (450ms) = select it (others fade, Cancel / Delete replace the title; tap elsewhere or Esc cancels); Delete removes the project (no undo). A 48px round **⋯** button (`#home-more`, `icons/more.svg`, "Icon button small") sits top-right level with the title (`--title-mid`) and opens the **backup menu**: a cream card (picker-card look) with "Back up projects" / "Restore a backup" chips and a note "Last backup: …" (`counter:last-backup`) or "Not backed up yet"; outside tap or Esc closes it; hidden while a ball is selected. **Back up** saves `{ app: "ribbit", version: 1, exportedAt, projects }` (non-blank projects) as `ribbit-backup-YYYY-MM-DD.json` via the share sheet (`navigator.share` with a file — on iPhone: Save to Files / iCloud) or else a download; cancelling the sheet doesn't count as a backup. **Restore** picks a JSON file and merges: unknown ids are added, known ids are replaced only if the file's copy has a newer `updatedAt`; nothing is removed; projects are tidied like stored ones (`importedProject` → `cleanProject`); the note reports "Restored: N added, M updated", "Everything in that backup is already here." or "That file isn't a Ribbit backup." The counter's back pill returns to Home. The off-screen view is made `inert`.

- **Main area (`#increment`, pointer handling in `app.js`):** tap the right half = +1, tap the left half = −1 (never below 0). Swipe down = **Enter** (adds the current count as a new row on top of the dots pile; the count stays as it is, so it becomes the default for the next row); swipe up = **Reset** (count → 0). While swiping, the number follows the finger 1:1 (CSS `translate`); it commits past 80px or on a flick, otherwise springs back (at 0 it moves with resistance and never commits). Keyboard activation = +1.
- **Feedback on counting taps:** each +1 / −1 plays a mechanical-keyboard click (Web Audio, synthesized — no sound files: a sharp band-passed noise click then ~12ms later a low "bottom-out" clack; +1 clicks at 4.2kHz, −1 is the same sound pitched down to 0.57× — a 2.4kHz click and a deeper clack; quiet) and a light haptic tick. The audio session is "ambient", so iOS mutes it on silent. Haptics: `navigator.vibrate` on Android; on iPhone (no vibration API) a hidden `<input type="checkbox" switch>` (`.haptic`) is toggled to get the system tick (iOS 18+, unofficial — only works inside a user gesture, fails silently).
- **Drop sound (swipe down / Enter):** `dropSound` — "pop-tok", picked by the user from an audition of six: a soft cork-like pop (a tone falling 900→220Hz in ~35ms, plus a breath of 1.5kHz noise), then ~45ms later a wooden "tok" (a 520→330Hz triangle tone plus a 2.2kHz click). Rejected along the way: rising per-dot ticks ("too spring-y"), a typewriter return with bell, a big-key thock. Same silent-switch rule; no haptic.
- **Button taps:** every other `<button>` click (back, title, colour circle, ×s, Cancel/Delete, swatches, project balls, +, rename ✓/×, …) plays `tapSound` — a soft iOS-keyboard-like tick with some body (made fuller after the first version felt too dry/short): a 1.5kHz band of noise (22ms), a 950→680Hz triangle tone ringing ~40ms, and a faint 300→180Hz thump; ~45ms total, quieter than the counting click. One capture-phase `click` listener on `document`, skipping `#increment` (the counter area has its own sounds). No haptic.
- Nothing else (drags in the colour panel, long-presses, the backdrop) has sound or haptic.
- **Layout:** default view = floating pills at the top (round back-arrow pill top-left `#back`, project-title pill top-centre, and a 48px circle filled with the dot colour, with the usual chunky border, top-right `#color-open`, which opens the colour picker), the big count's digits centred between the title pill's bottom and the top row of dots (`#increment`'s padding sets the space; `#count` is lifted 0.067em because Sigmar's digits sit below the middle of their line box — measure the ink, not the box; tried at the top half's centre, the user wanted it much lower), the dots strip on the bottom edge. The pills fade out in history mode (replaced by a single round close **×** button, `.bar--history` — the 48px cream "Icon button small" with `icons/x.svg`, not a primary button, hidden with `visibility` so `app.js` can always measure where the open strip stops) and while rows are selected (replaced by the same round × at the top centre, which then cancels the selection).
- **Dots strip:** shows the last 8 rows (raised from 3 at the user's request; `--visible-rows` in CSS / `VISIBLE_ROWS` in app.js; newest on top, nearest the count; rows are rendered newest-first and the strip is kept scrolled to the top; a fade at the bottom edge hints at older rows). Dots are rounded squares (12px, 3px corner radius, 8px gap — the word "dots" is kept throughout), always the same size; rows wider than the screen run off both edges and the whole strip scrolls horizontally (rows are wrapped in `#dot-rows`, sized to the widest row so all rows share one center line). The strip starts horizontally centered (on the squares only) and re-centers on each Enter; double-tap on the dots (either mode) glides them back to center. Scrolling is never diagonal: two nested one-axis scrollers — `.dots` scrolls vertically (history mode only), `#dot-scroller` horizontally — and the browser latches each gesture to one of them. (Don't toggle `overflow` mid-gesture to fake an axis lock: on iOS that made the group jump when off-centre.) Horizontal swipes scroll natively (`touch-action: pan-x`), vertical ones drive the open/close drag or history scrolling.
- **Group markers:** runs of consecutive rows with the same count are a group (always shown — the app must always signal how many rows each group has and how many squares each row has). Each group gets a 1px brown line 8px to the right of its squares, spanning from the top of its first row to the bottom of its last, and a label (Geist 500, 14px) 8px beside the line's bottom: "13x4" (squares x rows) or just "7" for a single row. A different count starts a new group. In the closed strip (only the newest 8 rows in view), a group that starts in view but continues below gets its label lifted beside its last visible row (`--lift`), dropping back to the real bottom in history mode. Markers are extra: they never affect centring — only the squares are centred (the user's rule). **Goalpost tag (`renderTags` / `drawTag` / `updateTags`):** the strip stays centred by default, so on long rows a group's marker can be off screen; then a tag pinned to that screen edge shows its label, pointed toward the marker. **Only one at a time:** the group nearest the current (newest) line, among the groups whose label is in the strip's view, whose marker is off screen (a label past the right edge, or a line past the left edge — then the tag is mirrored and pinned left). Look: 28px tall, Geist 600 16px, one SVG outline (`drawTag`: a fully round end and a 12px point — drawn as a single path so the point joins the top and bottom lines without gaps; an earlier rotated-square point left seams), cream fill, 1px brown line, its own soft shadow so it reads over the squares it covers (fine, per the user), tip 8px from the edge. Tags live in `#dot-tags`, inside `.dots` but outside the sideways scroller: they scroll up/down with the rows but stay on the edge; each is centred on its label's row but never above the strip's top (`--y`, or the top row's tag is clipped) and follows the label's `--lift`. Tapping the tag glides the strip until its marker is 16px inside the edge (double-tap the dots re-centres, as before); once the marker is in view the tag fades away. Updated on sideways and vertical scroll and strip resizing, on the next frame plus once 150ms after scrolling stops (a smooth glide's last frame could otherwise leave it stale). Tag taps don't reach the dots' tap/long-press/drag handlers. Built by `groupMark` in `renderDots`, hung (absolutely positioned) off each group's bottom row; `#dot-rows` has 56px side padding so the labels fit and the rows stay centred. Not shown in the Home previews. (Earlier, short segments on both sides of every row, joined for equal rows, were removed; these replaced them.)
- **History mode:** dragging up on the dots strip grows it 1:1 with the finger (custom pointer-event drag in `app.js`; the strip is anchored to the bottom edge and kept scrolled to the newest rows so they stay under the finger while older rows appear below; rubber-band past the available height). The big number fades out and blurs in proportion to the pull (`--pull` 0→1 on `<body>`; opacity reaches 0 at ~60% of the pull, so it's gone in history mode — the user found the dim blurred number still showing there bothersome). Released past 48px (or flicked up) it settles open as `body.is-history`: expanded up to just below the close × button, natively scrollable, taps don't count, the close **×** button fades in at the top; otherwise it springs back. The × (or Esc) animates back to the default view. Pushing the dots back down while the newest rows are showing also closes it: the strip shrinks 1:1 with the finger (touch events, so native scrolling through older rows still works) and closes past 48px or on a downward flick, otherwise springs back open. Mouse wheel down on the strip opens it; wheel up past the newest rows closes it. Available whenever there is at least 1 row (with few rows the strip can't grow, so opening is judged on pull distance and the number fades/blurs by distance).
- **Selecting (long-press a row, 450ms, either mode):** the **selected squares** get a cream card behind them (**row editor**, `#row-editor`, fades in), drawn over where they are in the strip at the same size — no zoom (a 1.5× pop-out was tried and removed at the user's request). The rows stay in the strip underneath; excluded squares are not dimmed or hidden. Purpose: a selection for bulk actions (colour, delete). The selection is a band of rows (`r1..r2`, display order) × a band of columns: rows are centred on one axis, so a square's place is its centre's offset from the axis, and it's selected when that offset is within `e1..e2`. **Grips** (free-standing knobs just outside the card's edges — a 14px cream disc with a 6px brown dot, a faint brown ring and its own shadow, centred 10px out so a 3px gap separates it from the card, and it stands out over the card, parchment or neighbouring squares alike; were short bars, then plain dots on the edge, changed at the user's request; `touch-action: none`; 44px touch areas that reach outward from the card — 36px out, 8px in — so the four never overlap, even on a one-row or one-square selection): left/right move `e1`/`e2` one square (20px) at a time — one square more/fewer in every selected row, whatever its length; top/bottom add/drop a row (24px), only within rows currently in view in the strip. Top/bottom grips are centred on the first/last row's segment; each side grip sits on the card's edge, halfway down the (first unbroken run of) rows that reach furthest that way — so on a stepped card it never floats beside a narrower row. A soft tick (`tapSound`) per change. It starts as the long-pressed row, trimmed from whichever end sticks out further until the card fits on screen. The card is one segment per selected row hugging its selected squares by 4px on every side (wider side padding was tried, but it covered the neighbouring excluded squares; the grips sit on the card's edges), segments meeting halfway across the row gaps, only outer corners rounded (7px), one shared drop shadow — equal rows make a rectangle, different lengths a stepped shape. Everything is positioned in `#dot-rows`' own coordinates and the selected squares are placed instantly exactly over the rows' own (never animated — animating them from a centred layout made left-edge changes look wrong). While selecting, the top pills give way to the round **×** at the top centre (`#done`, history mode's close button; here it cancels the selection — in history mode the first × cancels the selection, the next closes history; the Cancel/Delete bar was removed at the user's request), and two 48px chunky round buttons appear either side of it in the top row (`.select-actions`, 12px from the × — moved there from the bottom right at the user's request): on the left a circle filled with the selection's colour (`--selection`: its first square's) that opens the colour picker for the selected squares (`#select-color`), and on the right a red delete button with a cream bin icon (`#select-delete`, `icons/trash.svg`). **Delete removes the selected squares** from each selected row (rows left empty are removed; the remaining squares keep their own colours). **Moving the selection (keeps its size — w squares across, the column band's width, × h rows; `placeSelection`):** long-pressing any row (also inside the card) always selects that whole row; a tap on any square outside the card moves the selection there, centred on the tapped square (for an even w, the tapped square is the left of the middle two), sliding inward at a row's end rather than shrinking, and — if the tapped row is shorter than w — taking the whole row, which becomes the new size; dragging from inside the card (`touch-action: none`, tracked on window plus pointer capture on the card, since its segments are rebuilt while moving and a quick first move was otherwise lost) moves it square by square / row by row with a tick per step, keeping the size from the start of the drag (a short row passed over takes its whole row; the size returns on longer rows); a block of rows stays within the rows in view. Tapping the card itself does nothing. Tapping the main area (or Esc) cancels without counting, and the open/close drags are disabled. Delete keeps the scroll position; in history mode the strip re-fits, or closes if no rows remain. No undo. Rows carry `data-index` (their position in `entries`).
- **Projects (auto-saved):** every pile belongs to a project `{ id, name, updatedAt, entries, colors, color? }` (`entries` = one count per row, `colors` = each row's dot colour alongside it — a hex, or an array with one hex per square once part of a row has been recoloured through a selection (`squareColors` expands, `packColors` collapses back to one hex when all match), `color` = colour for new rows); every change (enter, delete row, rename, colour) is saved immediately — there is no Save button. The title pill shows the current project's name (default "New project"); tap it to rename (see Rename below). Projects are listed, opened, created and deleted on Home (above). An untouched project (no dots, default name) is never shown on Home and is discarded when you go back Home; **+** while on one reopens it. Deleting the current project switches to the most recently updated one (or a fresh project). On first run, the old `counter:entries` pile and `counter:designs` snapshots are migrated into projects and the old keys removed.
- **Rename (`#rename`, Figma node `121:4266`):** tapping the title pill opens an opaque layer (parchment `#f8f0e2` with blue `#7fa8c9` fading in towards the bottom — opacity ∝ (distance down)², matched to the Figma render) with the question "What shall we call this?" (Sigmar 40/40, max-width 261px so it breaks after "shall") and, under it, the name field + a 48px round button (Figma "Icon button small"). The field is focused straight from the tap so the system keyboard comes up, and the panel sits 16px above it (`--kb` from `visualViewport`). The field starts empty with the current name as placeholder (brown at 64%); typed text is full brown, the caret is the button green. The button is × (cancel) while empty, ✓ (save) once something is typed. ✓ / Enter saves (whitespace collapsed, max 40 chars), × / Esc cancels, dismissing the keyboard (blur) saves whatever was typed.
- **Dot colour (`#color-picker`):** each project has a current dot colour (`color`, hex; default brown `#5c5348`) shown by the top-right circle (`--dot` on `<body>`). Picking a colour only affects rows entered from then on: each row stores the colour it was entered with (`colors`, kept in step with `entries` on enter/delete) and is drawn with its own `--dot` (per square when it has several), in the strip and in the Home previews. Opened from a selection's colour button the same picker recolours **only the selected squares** (live, saved on release like other picks; `colorSelection`) and leaves the colour for new rows alone; it starts from the selection's colour; the selection stays after the picker closes. Rows saved before per-row colours existed get the project colour they were showing (`cleanProject`). Tapping the circle opens a full-width card under the top pills (16px gutters): a saturation (x) / brightness (y) gradient panel and a rainbow hue strip, each with a white-ringed thumb, then a small "In this project:" label (Geist 14px) over a row of swatches — one per distinct colour used in this project's squares (most recent first), drawn as 34px circles with a flat 1px brown border (no depth) in 44px tap areas; tapping one picks that exact colour, and the matching swatch gets a ring; label and swatches hidden when there are no rows. No confirm button: drag or tap to pick (live preview on the circle; arrow keys nudge, Shift = ×10), and each pick is saved straight away (on release, swatch tap or arrow key). Close with an outside tap on the transparent backdrop (never counts), the circle again, or Esc — the picked colour stays. The colour changes only when the user picks one, so the no-flashing rule still holds.
- Opening a project sets the count to that project's last row's number (0 if it has no rows); the count itself isn't stored.
- Projects persist in `localStorage` (`counter:projects` — all projects; `counter:current-project` — id of the open one). The old shared-count key `counter:value` is removed on startup. Storage is per address (origin): the hosted app, `localhost` and a LAN IP each have their own projects; on iPhone a home-screen app also has its own storage, separate from Safari's, deleted with the icon — hence the backup menu. `navigator.storage.persist()` is requested on startup.
- **Installable (PWA):** `manifest.webmanifest` (name Ribbit, `display: standalone`, parchment theme/background, icons 192/512 + maskable 512) plus the `apple-mobile-web-app-*` tags and `apple-touch-icon` in `index.html` — Add to Home Screen opens it full screen without browser bars. Status bar style `default` + the parchment `theme-color`: checked on the user's iPhone (looks right, app is full page). `sw.js` (service worker, registered only over http(s)) precaches the app files (`SHELL`) and serves network-first (`cache: "no-cache"`), falling back to the saved copy offline, so updates show as soon as the phone is online. Adding a file the app loads → add it to `SHELL` in `sw.js` and to `build.sh`; bump `CACHE` (`ribbit-vN`) only if the cached copy must be dropped.
- **Hosting:** live at **https://ribbit.sofia-secchi-7.workers.dev** — a Cloudflare **Worker** (static assets only; Cloudflare account sofia.secchi.7) connected to the GitHub repo, deploying on every push to `main`. Cloudflare's build runs `sh build.sh` (copies the app's files into `dist/`) and then `npx wrangler deploy`, which publishes `dist/` as set in `wrangler.jsonc`. Keep that file: without it Cloudflare generates one that publishes the whole repo (CLAUDE.md, serve.py, .claude/ — this happened on the first deploy). The host redirects `/index.html` → `/`, so `sw.js` caches `./`, never `index.html`.

Under git, hosted in a **private** GitHub repo: `git@github.com:sofiasecchi7-spec/ribbit.git` (branch `main`). That's how the work moves between machines, so the folder must stay self-contained. Commit and push only when the user asks; pull before starting work on a machine.

## Setting up on a new machine (Claude: do this when asked to set up or run)

1. Get the code: `git clone git@github.com:sofiasecchi7-spec/ribbit.git` (needs the machine's SSH key added to the GitHub account; check with `ssh -T git@github.com`). If it's already cloned, `git pull`.
2. Check the prerequisites below are installed; install any that are missing (ask the user first if it needs `sudo` or a password).
3. Run the install steps.
4. Run the app with the run command and confirm it works (e.g. `curl -s localhost:4422 | grep -q '<title>Ribbit'`, and `curl -sI localhost:4422/styles.css | grep -i no-store`).

### Prerequisites
- A web browser. That's all for the app itself — it's plain HTML/CSS/JS with no build step and no dependencies.
- Optional, for serving (needed to test on a phone): `python3` (any 3.x). Check with `python3 --version`. On macOS, if missing, `xcode-select --install` provides it.

### Install
Nothing to install.

### Run
- Quickest: open `index.html` in a browser (works via `file://`).
- Served (from this folder): `python3 serve.py` (optional port argument, default 4422) → http://localhost:4422
  - `serve.py` is `python3 -m http.server` plus `Cache-Control: no-store` (standard library only), bound to `0.0.0.0`. Use it rather than plain `http.server`: without the header, Safari on a phone kept showing old CSS/JS after changes.
  - If port 4422 is taken, pick another free port (`python3 serve.py 4423`) and tell the user which.
  - In the Claude desktop app, `.claude/launch.json` defines a `counter` preview config that runs `serve.py`.
- On a phone (same Wi-Fi): run `python3 serve.py`, then open `http://<computer's LAN IP>:4422`. Get the IP on macOS with `ipconfig getifaddr en0`.

### Secrets / environment variables
None. Never store secrets in this folder; load them from 1Password or environment variables on each machine.

## Files
- `index.html` — markup
- `styles.css` — styles, written mobile-first
- `fonts/sigmar-latin.woff2` — Sigmar (display: "Ribbit" title, the big count), Latin subset, SIL Open Font License.
- `fonts/geist-latin.woff2` — Geist variable font (all other text, weights 500–700), Latin subset, SIL Open Font License. Fonts are loaded via `@font-face` in `styles.css` and bundled so no network is needed.
- `icons/plus.svg`, `icons/x.svg`, `icons/check.svg` — icons exported from the Figma file; `icons/more.svg` — the ⋯ (backup menu) icon.
- `icons/app-icon.svg` — app icon source (a project ball with rows of dots on Home's background; content inside the central 80% so it also works maskable); `icons/apple-touch-icon.png` (180), `icons/icon-192.png`, `icons/icon-512.png` are rendered from it (no image tools on the Mac: drawn to a canvas in the browser and saved).
- `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js` — installable app + offline copy (see Installable).
- `build.sh` — hosting build: copies the app's files into `dist/` (git-ignored) for Cloudflare.
- `wrangler.jsonc` — Cloudflare Worker config: name `ribbit`, publishes `./dist` only (see Hosting).
- `serve.py` — the local dev server (see Run): static files with no caching (and the right type for `.webmanifest`). Standard library only.
- `app.js` — counter logic (plain script, not an ES module, so `file://` keeps working). Toggles `.bump` on `#count` for the tap animation; handles all gestures (taps, swipes, dots drag, axis lock, double-tap).

## Conventions
- **Visual style (from the Ribbit Figma file; tokens on `:root` in `styles.css`):** one static parchment background `#f4ebd9`; display text black (Sigmar); UI text, strokes and dots brown `#5c5348` (dots are per-project, see Dot colour); project balls cream like the buttons; buttons are "chunky" — 1px border with a 3px bottom edge that sinks on press — cream `#faf4e6` (pills, chips), green `#4a9c3e`/`#2f6b27` (+), red (Delete). Fonts **Sigmar** and **Geist** bundled in `fonts/`; no instructional text on screen.
- **No flashing colours (hard rule):** the background must not change colour on taps or counts — no per-tap hue shifts, no even/odd alternation. The user found that visually noisy and a photosensitivity (epilepsy) risk. Motion feedback (bump, drag-follow) is fine; colour flashes are not.
- **Screen margins: 16px at the top and bottom (user's request)** — Home's title and the counter's top pills/× / Cancel-Delete bar sit 16px from the top, the + and the dots strip 16px from the bottom — always as `max(16px, env(safe-area-inset-*))`, so on a phone with a home indicator or notch the safe area wins.
- **Mobile-first:** base CSS targets small screens; add larger-screen styles only inside `@media (min-width: …)`. Keep tap targets ≥ 44px (bar buttons are 56px), respect `env(safe-area-inset-*)`, use `100dvh` not `100vh`. The controls are deliberately at the top (user's choice) — the bottom edge belongs to the dots strip.
- Test at a ~375px-wide viewport first.

## Rules for keeping this portable (Claude: follow on every change)

- Whenever you add a runtime, dependency, script, port, or env var, update the sections above in the same step.
- If dependencies are ever added, keep the lockfile (`package-lock.json`, `requirements.txt`, …) in the folder; never rely on `node_modules`, `.venv`, or build output being present — they are reinstalled per machine.
- Use relative paths only; no `/Users/<name>/...` paths.
- Pin runtime versions where possible (e.g. `.nvmrc`, `.python-version`).
